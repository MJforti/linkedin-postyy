## Post Content

A Fortune 500 retail bank just pulled the plug on a $14.2M modernization contract with their offshore IT vendor.

The vendor had quoted 48 engineers and 9 months just to migrate an internal payments module.

Instead, a 3-person internal engineering pod delivered the working core in 11 days using Claude, Cursor, and automated test harnesses.

The entire billable-hour model behind enterprise IT services is coming undone in plain sight.

For 25 years, IT outsourcing lived on one simple margin engine: hire thousands of engineers at $25 an hour, bill enterprise accounts at $95 an hour, and let timelines stretch to keep invoice volume high.

Hiring scale was the whole moat. More engineers sitting on a bench meant more revenue on the quarterly report.

CTOs auditing their vendor roster this quarter are looking at the exact same friction:
- A 14-person manual QA team costs 50x more than automated synthetic testing running on a $200 server.
- Legacy system migrations that used to justify six months of "discovery" can be mapped in 48 hours by local LLMs.
- Billing 40 hours a week for junior developers to write boilerplate APIs is slower than one senior engineer with modern tooling.

The real trap for legacy consultancies is their own commercial model.

If an IT shop uses AI agents to finish a client's modernization project in two weeks instead of nine months, their billable hours drop by 90%.

They can't afford to be fast, because speed directly cannibalizes their top line.

Enterprise buyers have finally realized that paying for developer headcount was never the same thing as buying shipped software.

If you lead an engineering department or manage IT vendors, how many billable seats on your current contracts exist solely because the agency's pricing model punishes automation?

P.S. For anyone working inside enterprise IT services, are your clients already pushing for mandatory AI productivity discounts on renewals?
