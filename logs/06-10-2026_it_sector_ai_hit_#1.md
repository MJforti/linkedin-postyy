## Post Content

A Fortune 500 bank just canceled a $14.2M IT services contract with an offshore vendor.

The vendor had quoted 48 engineers and 9 months to migrate an internal payments module.

A 3-person internal engineering pod delivered the working core in 11 days using Claude and automated test harnesses.

The IT services sector isn't facing a temporary tech slowdown. It is facing a structural collapse of the billable hour.

For 25 years, the entire IT outsourcing model was built on a simple arbitrage: hire thousands of smart engineers at $25/hour, bill enterprise clients at $95/hour, and let project timelines stretch to maximize invoice volume.

Hiring scale was the moat. More bodies on a bench meant more revenue on the balance sheet.

That math is now broken.

Enterprise CTOs are auditing their vendor roster and realizing three brutal truths:
- A 14-person manual QA team can be replaced by continuous synthetic testing running on a $200 server.
- Legacy migrations that once justified 6-month discovery phases can be mapped in 48 hours by local LLMs.
- Junior developers billing 40 hours a week to write boilerplate APIs are now slower than a senior architect using Cursor.

The existential trap for legacy IT firms is their own revenue model.

If an IT consultancy uses AI to solve a client's problem in two weeks instead of nine months, their billable hours drop by 90%.

They cannot afford to be fast because speed cannibalizes their top line.

Clients are no longer buying developer hours. They are buying shipped software.

If you lead an IT team or consult for enterprise clients, how many seats on your current contracts exist solely because the billing model punishes automation?

P.S. If you work in enterprise IT services, are your clients demanding AI productivity discounts on their renewals yet?
